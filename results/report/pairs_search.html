What it means

  • The search found a setting that made money in 1999-2012 (Sharpe +0.64, monthly t 2.7) and it lost money in 2013-2026 (Sharpe -0.25). The luck checks said so in advance: the Reality Check could not tell the best of 960 from chance (p 0.29), the Deflated Sharpe was 0.30 against the 0.95 needed, and random unrelated pairs produced an equally good best result 24% of the time.
  • Pairs trading paid a little for most settings in 1999-2012, mostly in 1999 and the 2008-09 crisis, then stopped: 78% of settings were positive in the search, 21% in the holdout. This matches the published decline of pairs profits after 2002.
  • The search ranking carried a little information (rank correlation +0.20), mostly about what to avoid (20-day lookbacks, 5-day holds, entering at a 1.0 gap), but even the search's top tenth lost money in the holdout.
  • Re-tuning every January did worse (Sharpe -0.37): each setting it picked did worse in the years it was picked than in the other years. That is chasing last period's winner.
  • The selected rule did no better on its same-industry pairs than on random unrelated pairs (median -0.21), so the twin relationship added nothing in the holdout.
  • The holdout's own best settings (table below) were middling in the search, and much of their gain came from 2020. Picking them now would repeat the mistake this test was built to catch; they would need a fresh test on data from 2027 on, or paper trading.
