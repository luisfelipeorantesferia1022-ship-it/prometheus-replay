Does the tight-spread rule hold up on new data?

Pre-registered test on 2024 and January-September 2026, real OPRA quotes (Databento), every exit simulated minute by minute. Generated from results/oos/verdict.json and oos_trades.csv.

The question

In 2025, options bought with a bid-ask spread under 5% at entry made money under the live V3 sell rules, but that 5% line was drawn on the same 2025 data. This page is the test of the unchanged rule on two periods it had never seen, scored against criteria written down before the data was downloaded (results/oos/PREREGISTRATION.md).

Rule
EntryFirst trading day of each week, 9:46 ET, at the ask
ContractThe agent's pick: 12-24 days to expiry (14-21 preferred), strike nearest 1% out of the money; both the call and the put
QualifiesSpread under 5% of the mid and delta at least 0.35
ExitLive V3 rules every minute: stop -75%, ceiling +200%, trail 20 points after +50%, force exit 13:00 the day before expiry; trigger on the mid, sell at the bid
StocksEach week the 40 S&P 500 members with the most dollar volume over the prior 20 days, plus 10 random members

What it means

  • The 2025 result did not carry over. On new data, options with spreads under 5% roughly broke even under the V3 sell rules (+1.4% in 2024 on 2,066 trades, +0.1% in 2026 on 642), and both 90% ranges include zero. The +10.8% of 2025 (86 trades) looks like an upward fluke of the search that found it.
  • Tight spreads still beat wide ones by 7-13 points, so the cost effect is real: paying a wide spread loses money, paying a tight one only gets you to break-even.
  • Calls made money and puts lost in both periods. That reflects which way the stocks drifted during the holds, the market's direction rather than anything the rule controls, and it would flip in a falling market.
  • Caveat on 2026: entry spreads in the 2026 data are much wider than in 2024 for the same names (median $0.20 vs $1.20 per contract-share), so fewer options qualified. It has not been checked whether that is the real market or a quirk of the quote data. 2024's quotes look normal and fail the test on their own, so the verdict does not depend on 2026.
