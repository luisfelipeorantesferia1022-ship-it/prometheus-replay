The question and the data

Can anything known at 9:46 on the entry morning pick out the winners, or at least weed out the losers? Searched on the 1,518 real-option trades of the calm-stock test (both the call and the put for each stock-week, bought at the ask at 9:46, followed to the Thursday exit). A pattern only counts if it shows up the same way in January-June and in July-December.

The one thing that held up: the bid-ask spread at entry

Spread = (ask - bid) / mid at 9:46. The agent already skips spreads over 20%. Tighter is better in both halves of the year. Under 5% spread these were mostly mega-caps (GOOGL, BAC, GS, NVDA, META, MSFT); only 86 options, so the break-even there is uncertain (90% range roughly -10% to +12%).

What it means

  • Nothing knowable at 9:46 picks winners. The LSTM measures, volatility, beta, implied vol, earnings timing, momentum, RSI, gaps and volume all failed to show up the same way in both halves of 2025.
  • The only reliable loser filter is cost: the wider the spread you pay, the more duds. That is arithmetic, not prediction: bought at the ask and sold at the bid, a 30% spread has to be made back before any profit.
  • Following SPY's trend (the agent's own direction rule) and fading the opening gap worked strongly in January-June, which held the April crash and rebound, and almost not at all in July-December.
  • Calls beat puts in both halves, but 2025 was a rising year; that would reverse in a falling one.
  • Even the best bucket (spread under 5%) only about broke even with a +50% take-profit.
